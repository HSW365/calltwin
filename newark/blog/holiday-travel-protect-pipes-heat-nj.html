Going Away for the Holidays? Protect Your Pipes and Heat in Warren County

Thanksgiving and the December holidays are when a lot of homes and businesses in Warren, Hunterdon, Sussex, Morris and Somerset counties sit empty for days at a time, right as the nights turn cold. A furnace that quits or a pipe that freezes while nobody is home can turn into a flooded basement before anyone notices. A little preparation before you leave goes a long way.

Why an empty building is at higher risk

When you're home, you notice problems early. The house feels cold, you hear water running, you see a drip under the sink. When you're away, the same problem keeps going until you get back or a neighbor spots it.

Frozen pipes are the big one. A pipe in an outside wall, a crawl space, an unheated garage or a basement corner can freeze if the heat drops or fails. Water expands as it freezes, and the pipe can split. Often the leak doesn't start until the ice thaws, which means water can run for hours or days in an empty house. And if the furnace, boiler or heat pump shuts down while you're gone, every vulnerable pipe is exposed.

Before you leave: heat

  • Don't turn the heat off. Lower it if you like, but keep it set high enough that the coldest parts of the house stay well above freezing. Outside walls and basements run colder than the thermostat's room.
  • Replace thermostat batteries. If your thermostat uses batteries, a dead one can stop the heat while you're away. Swap them before you go.
  • Check the filter. A clogged furnace or heat pump filter can make the system run long or shut itself off. Change it if it's dirty.
  • Make sure the heat actually works. If the system has been short cycling, making new noises, or struggling to keep up, get it looked at before you leave, not after.
  • Oil or propane heat? Check the tank level and schedule a delivery if it's getting low. Running out while you're away means no heat.
  • Leave interior doors open. Open doors to rooms and cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so warm air can reach the pipes.

Before you leave: water

  • Know where your main shutoff is. The main water valve is usually where the water line enters the house, often in the basement near the front wall, or near the well tank if you're on a well.
  • Consider shutting the water off. For a longer trip, closing the main valve and opening a faucet on the lowest floor to relieve pressure limits how much water can leak if a pipe breaks. If you do this, think about your water heater and any boiler or fire sprinkler system first (see below).
  • Check the sump pump. Pour some water into the sump pit and confirm the pump starts and the water drains away.
  • Look for small leaks now. A slow drip is worth fixing before a week of nobody watching it.

Things to be careful with

A few systems need water or power even when you're away. Call if you're not sure.

  • Hot water boilers. Many boilers refill automatically from the house water supply. If you shut the main water off, a boiler that loses pressure may not be able to refill. If you heat with a boiler, ask before closing the main valve for a long trip.
  • Water heaters. Many gas water heaters have a vacation setting that keeps the pilot lit at a low temperature. Check your owner's manual. Don't drain a water heater or turn off the gas yourself unless you know how to safely relight and refill it.
  • Fire sprinkler systems. A sprinkler system has to stay live to protect the building, so never shut off its supply valve. In some homes the sprinklers are fed from the same main as the plumbing, and closing the main would take them out of service. If you have sprinklers, ask before shutting off water, and keep the heat on wherever sprinkler pipes run.
  • Well pumps. If you're on a well, turning off the pump breaker stops water flow, but make sure that won't affect a boiler, a geothermal system or anything else that depends on it.

Have someone check on the house

The best protection is a person. Ask a neighbor, friend or family member to stop in every day or two. Show them where the main water shutoff is, give them New Ark's number, and ask them to look for:

  • A house that feels colder than it should, or a thermostat reading well below where you set it.
  • Water on floors, stains on ceilings, or the sound of running water.
  • Frost or ice on exposed pipes in the basement or crawl space.
  • Any alarm sounding, including smoke or carbon monoxide alarms.

Businesses closing for the holidays

Offices, shops, churches and other commercial buildings that close for a long weekend face the same risks. Before closing, confirm the heat is on in every zone, especially stairwells, back rooms, loading areas and any space with sprinkler piping or plumbing on an outside wall. Make sure someone has keys, knows where the water shutoff and sprinkler valves are, and has a number to call.

If something goes wrong

If you or your house-sitter find water coming from a broken pipe, shut off the main water valve right away, then shut off power to any wet area if you can do it safely without standing in water. Then call.

If the house is cold and the heat won't come back on, keep cabinet doors open, don't use an open flame to warm pipes, and call for heating repair before the pipes freeze.

If anyone smells gas, don't flip switches, make calls from inside, or try to find the leak. Get everyone out of the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get into fresh air and call 911.
