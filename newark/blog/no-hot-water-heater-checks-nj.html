No Hot Water? Water Heater Checks for Warren and Hunterdon County Homes This Winter

Cold weather is when a water heater works hardest. The water coming into the house is colder, everyone wants a hot shower, and holiday guests mean more dishes, laundry and baths. If you turn on the tap and get nothing but cold, or the hot water runs out far faster than it used to, here are the safe checks to make first and the signs that it's time to call a plumber.

Safety first

If you smell gas anywhere near the water heater or anywhere in the house, don't try to relight anything, don't flip light switches, and don't use your phone inside. Leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Call a plumber only after the utility says it's safe.

Also stay away if there's water on the floor near an electric water heater or the electrical panel, or if you see scorch marks, smell burning, or hear hissing from the relief valve on the side of the tank. Those are calls for help, not do-it-yourself checks.

First, figure out how big the problem is

Try the hot water at more than one faucet, including one on a different floor. The answer points you in the right direction:

  • No hot water anywhere: the problem is usually at the water heater itself, such as the gas supply, the pilot or burner, a tripped breaker or a failed part.
  • Hot water at some fixtures but not others: the issue may be a single faucet, shower valve or mixing valve rather than the heater.
  • Hot water that runs out fast or is only lukewarm: the heater may be undersized for winter demand, have sediment built up in the tank, have a failed heating element, or have a thermostat that needs adjusting.

Checks for a gas water heater

  • Look at the gas control on the front of the heater. Make sure the knob hasn't been bumped to OFF or PILOT, and that the gas shutoff valve on the supply line is open (handle in line with the pipe).
  • Check whether other gas appliances in the house, like the stove or furnace, are working. If none of them are, the issue may be with the gas supply, and your gas utility is the right first call.
  • Many water heaters have a small window near the bottom where you can see the pilot or burner. If it's out, the relighting instructions are on the label on the tank. Follow them exactly, and only if there is no gas smell. If the pilot won't stay lit, stop there. Repeated relighting attempts are a sign a part like the thermocouple or gas control needs a plumber.
  • Newer heaters may have a status light on the gas control that blinks a code. The label or the owner's manual explains what the pattern means. Write it down for the plumber.
  • Make sure nothing is stored against the heater or blocking the air openings near the bottom, and that the vent pipe on top is still connected. A heater needs air to burn safely, and a disconnected vent can let exhaust into the house.

Checks for an electric water heater

  • Go to the electrical panel and look for the water heater breaker. If it's tripped, you can reset it once, as long as the floor is dry and you're comfortable doing it. If it trips again, leave it off and call. A breaker that keeps tripping usually means a shorted element or a wiring problem.
  • Some electric heaters have a reset button behind a cover panel on the tank. Because those covers sit over live wiring, we recommend leaving that to a plumber unless you know how to cut power and work on it safely.
  • If you get some hot water but it runs out quickly, one of the two heating elements may have failed. That's a common repair, but it involves electrical work inside the tank and should be done by a professional.

When the hot water runs out too fast

In winter, the water entering the tank is much colder than in summer, so the heater needs more time to bring it up to temperature. A heater that kept up fine in July can struggle in December, especially with extra people in the house.

  • Space out showers, laundry and the dishwasher so the tank has time to recover.
  • If the tank rumbles, pops or crackles while heating, sediment has likely built up on the bottom. That reduces how much hot water you get and makes the heater work harder. Flushing or servicing the tank is a job a plumber can handle.
  • Don't turn the thermostat way up to compensate. Very hot water is a scald risk, especially for children and older adults. Ask your plumber about a safe setting for your household.

Signs it's time to call now

  • Any gas smell (leave first and call the gas utility or 911).
  • Water leaking from the tank, the fittings or the relief valve.
  • The pilot won't stay lit or the burner won't come on.
  • The breaker trips again after one reset.
  • Rusty or discolored hot water, or a rotten-egg smell from the hot water only.
  • A heater that's been struggling for a while and is getting older. That's a good time to talk about repair versus replacement before it fails completely.
