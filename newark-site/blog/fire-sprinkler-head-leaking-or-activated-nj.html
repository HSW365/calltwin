Fire Sprinkler Head Leaking or Went Off? What NJ Homes and Businesses Should Do

Water pouring from a ceiling sprinkler head, or a slow drip that wasn't there yesterday, is alarming, and it tends to happen at the worst time. This guide walks homeowners, property managers and business owners in Warren, Hunterdon, Sussex, Morris and Somerset counties through what to do in the first few minutes, what to leave alone, and how to get the system working again.

First: make sure there is no fire

A sprinkler head opens because heat reached it. Before you treat it as a plumbing problem, check that nothing is burning. Look for smoke, flames or a hot smell near the head that went off, and in the rooms around it.

If there is any sign of fire, get everyone out and call 911. Do not shut off the sprinkler water. The system is doing its job by holding the fire back until firefighters arrive, and turning it off can let a small fire grow fast.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Don't flip switches or try to find the source.

If there is no fire and water is flowing

A head that opened without a fire, or a fitting that has started to leak, can put out a lot of water quickly. Once you're sure there's no fire, these steps limit the damage:

  1. Find the sprinkler system's main control valve and close it. In many homes it's near where the water service enters the building, often in the basement or a utility room, and may be tagged. In commercial buildings it's usually in a riser room, sprinkler room or near the fire department connection. If you don't know where it is or can't reach it safely, call us and the fire department.
  2. If your system is monitored by an alarm company, call them and tell them what happened. A sprinkler flowing water often sends a signal, and the fire department may already be on the way. Let them in and explain that there is no fire.
  3. Stay away from water near outlets, electrical panels, lights and appliances. Turn off power at the breaker only if you can reach it while standing somewhere dry.
  4. Open the main drain or inspector's test valve only if you've been shown how and know where the water goes. Otherwise leave it for the technician.
  5. Move people, records, inventory and electronics away from the wet area, and start removing standing water.

If a head or fitting is just dripping

A slow drip is still a reason to call. It can mean a damaged head, a loose or corroded fitting, too much pressure in the system, or condensation on a pipe in a cold space. Put a bucket under it, take a photo of the head and the area around it, and note when you first noticed it.

Don't tighten, tap or wrap the head yourself, and don't try to plug it. Sprinkler heads are precise devices. Bumping or twisting a dripping head is one of the easiest ways to make it open fully.

Common reasons heads go off without a fire

Knowing why it happened helps you keep it from happening again. A few causes come up again and again, and several of them are more likely in late fall and winter:

  • Physical damage. A ladder, a box on a high shelf, a forklift or a piece of furniture hits the head and breaks the glass bulb or the fusible link.
  • Things hung from the head or pipe. Holiday decorations, lights, signs and hangers clipped to sprinkler heads or pipes put stress on parts that aren't made to carry weight.
  • Heat near the head. Space heaters, work lamps, cooking equipment or heaters pointed at the ceiling can raise the temperature at the head enough to set it off.
  • Freezing. Water in pipes running through unheated attics, garages, loading docks or exterior walls can freeze, expand and crack a head or fitting. The leak often shows up when it thaws.
  • Paint, corrosion or age. A head that has been painted over, or one that is corroded, may not work the way it should and should be checked.

What not to do

  • Don't shut off the system if there is any chance of fire. Call 911 first.
  • Don't replace a head yourself. Replacement heads have to match the system's temperature rating, type and coverage. The wrong head can leave part of the building unprotected.
  • Don't leave the system off longer than you have to. While the valve is closed, the building has no sprinkler protection. Get it repaired and back in service as soon as you can.
  • Don't paint, cover or hang anything from heads or pipes, even for a short time.

While the system is out of service

For a home, that means being extra careful with candles, space heaters and cooking until the system is back on, and keeping smoke alarms working. For a business, landlord or property manager, check with your fire department, insurance carrier and alarm company about what they expect while sprinklers are down, and tell your tenants or staff.

Once the cause is found and the damaged parts are replaced, the system needs to be refilled, checked for leaks and returned to service properly. That's work for a fire sprinkler contractor, not a general handyman.
