Avoiding a Clogged Kitchen Drain on Thanksgiving in Warren County, NJ

Thanksgiving puts more on your plumbing in one day than most weeks do: pans full of grease, piles of peels, a dishwasher that never stops and a bathroom line out the door. Here's how homeowners in Warren, Hunterdon, Sussex, Morris and Somerset counties can keep the kitchen drain flowing, and what to do if it backs up anyway.

Why holiday cooking clogs drains

Most kitchen clogs build up from the same few things: fats and grease, starchy food scraps, and fibrous peels. On a normal night a little goes down the sink and gets flushed through. On a holiday, a lot goes down in a few hours, and the line between your sink and the main drain gets more than it can carry.

Grease is the biggest problem. Turkey drippings, butter and gravy are liquid when they're hot, so they seem to pour away fine. As they cool inside the pipe they thicken and stick to the walls, and food scraps catch on that coating until the drain slows or stops.

What not to put down the sink or disposal

Keep these in the trash or compost instead:

  • Grease, oil, pan drippings and gravy. Let them cool in a can or jar, then throw them out.
  • Potato and other vegetable peels. They're starchy and can pack into a paste in the trap.
  • Celery, onion skins, corn husks and other stringy or fibrous scraps that can wrap around disposal parts.
  • Turkey bones and skin.
  • Rice, pasta, stuffing and bread, which swell with water.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells, which settle in the pipe like sand.

Scrape plates into the trash before rinsing or loading the dishwasher. It's the single easiest way to protect the drain on a busy day.

If you use a garbage disposal

  • Run cold water before, during and for a little while after you use it.
  • Feed it small amounts at a time instead of a whole cutting board of scraps.
  • Don't use it as the main way to get rid of holiday leftovers. It grinds food; it doesn't make grease or starch disappear.
  • If it hums but doesn't spin, or stops on its own, turn it off. Don't keep flipping the switch.

Spread out the water use

With a full house, the dishwasher, the sink, the washing machine and the showers can all be running at once. Everything drains to the same main line. If you can, run the dishwasher after dinner instead of during cooking, and hold off on laundry until guests leave.

If your home is on a septic system, as many homes in western New Jersey are, a big gathering puts a heavy load on it too. Spreading out water use and keeping grease out of the drains helps the system as well as the pipes.

In the bathroom, keep a trash can next to the toilet and make sure guests know to use it. Wipes, paper towels and other products should never be flushed, even ones labeled flushable.

If the kitchen sink clogs anyway

  1. Stop running water into that sink and turn off the dishwasher so you don't add more.
  2. Try a plain cup plunger on the sink. If you have a double sink, plug or hold a wet rag over the other drain so the pressure goes toward the clog.
  3. Don't pour chemical drain cleaners into a sink that's fully blocked. They can sit in standing water, damage pipes and splash on whoever works on the drain next.
  4. For a jammed disposal, switch it off and turn off its breaker or unplug it under the sink before doing anything. Never put your hand inside it. Many disposals have a reset button on the bottom of the unit.

If the sink still won't drain, it's time to call a plumber rather than keep forcing it.

Signs the problem is the main sewer line

A single slow sink is usually a local clog. When more than one fixture is affected, the blockage is usually further down, in the main drain or sewer line. Watch for:

  • Water coming up in a basement floor drain, tub or shower when the sink, dishwasher or washer runs.
  • Toilets gurgling or backing up when you use other fixtures.
  • Several drains slow at the same time.
  • A sewage smell in the basement.

If you see any of these, stop using water in the house, keep people away from any sewage, and call. A main line backup on a holiday is a real emergency, and it will only get worse as guests keep using the bathroom and kitchen.

Gas safety in a busy kitchen

If you smell gas at any point, whether from the stove, oven or anywhere else, don't try to find the source and don't switch lights or appliances on or off. Get everyone out of the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Call us after you're safe.
