Frozen Pipes in Warren County: What to Do Before the Plumber Arrives

You turn on the kitchen faucet on a bitter morning and get a trickle, or nothing at all. That usually means water has frozen somewhere in the line. A frozen pipe is not yet a flood, and what you do in the next hour can decide whether it stays that way. Here is what to do, and what not to do, while help is on the way in Warren, Hunterdon, Sussex, Morris and Somerset County.

How to tell a pipe is frozen

Frozen pipes usually show up on the coldest mornings, often after a night when the wind was blowing. Common signs:

  • One faucet or fixture has little or no water while others in the house work normally.
  • A toilet does not refill after flushing.
  • Frost on an exposed pipe, or a section of pipe that feels much colder than the rest.
  • The affected fixture is on an outside wall, over a crawl space, near a garage, or in an unheated basement or attic.

If no fixture in the building has water, the freeze may be near where the main line enters, or the problem may be with the water supply itself. Check with neighbors or your water utility before assuming it is your pipes.

Safety first

If you smell gas at any point, stop what you are doing, leave the building, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not switch lights or appliances on or off on your way out.

Water and electricity do not mix. If water is already dripping or pooling near outlets, a panel, extension cords or appliances, stay out of the wet area and keep everyone else out too. If you cannot reach your electrical panel safely without standing in water, do not try.

Step 1: Find your main water shutoff now

Before you try to thaw anything, locate the main shutoff valve, usually where the water line comes through the basement or crawl space wall, near the water meter, or in a utility area. Make sure you can reach it and that everyone in the house knows where it is.

A frozen pipe often has a split you cannot see. The ice is holding the water back. When it thaws, that split can start spraying. If you see any crack, bulge or leak, shut the main off right away. If you do not, keep your hand near it while the line thaws.

Step 2: Open the faucet

Open the faucet the frozen line feeds, both hot and cold if it is a mixing faucet. An open faucet relieves pressure in the pipe and lets you see when water starts moving again. A slow drip that grows into a steady stream is a good sign the ice is clearing.

Step 3: Add gentle, safe warmth

If you can see the frozen section and there is no sign of a crack or leak, you can try to warm it gently. Start closest to the faucet and work back toward the frozen area so melting water has somewhere to go.

  • Raise the thermostat and open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so household heat reaches the pipes.
  • Use a hair dryer on a low or warm setting, moving it back and forth along the pipe. Keep the dryer and its cord away from any water.
  • Wrap the pipe in towels soaked in warm water, and replace them as they cool.
  • Use a space heater only if it is in good condition, set on a dry, level floor, kept well away from anything that can burn, and never left unattended.

Be patient. Thawing can take a while. If the frozen section is inside a wall, ceiling or floor where you cannot reach it, do not cut into anything. Raise the heat in that room and call a plumber.

What never to do

  • Never use a torch, open flame, propane heater or charcoal heater to thaw a pipe. That is a fire and carbon monoxide hazard, and it can damage the pipe.
  • Do not run any fuel-burning heater indoors that is not designed for indoor use.
  • Do not pour boiling water on pipes or fittings.
  • Do not strike a frozen pipe to break up the ice.
  • Do not leave a heat source running near a pipe when no one is in the room.

If the pipe has already burst

  1. Shut off the main water valve immediately.
  2. If water is near electrical outlets, panels or appliances, stay clear and keep others away.
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain what is left in the lines.
  4. Move valuables, electronics and stored items out of the water's path.
  5. Take photos of the damage for your records.
  6. Call a plumber. Burst pipes are an emergency.

Help keep it from freezing again

Once water is flowing, the same pipe is likely to freeze again in the next cold snap unless something changes. Until it is fixed for good:

  • Let the faucet on that line drip slightly on very cold nights.
  • Keep cabinet doors open on outside walls during cold weather.
  • Keep the heat on, even in rooms you do not use and in homes or buildings that are empty for a while.
  • Close garage doors and seal drafts near exposed pipes where you safely can.

A plumber can look at why that section froze and recommend fixes such as insulating the pipe, sealing drafts, or rerouting a line away from a cold wall. If you have not done it yet, see our guide to winterizing outdoor faucets and pipes.

Businesses, rentals and fire sprinkler lines

Shops, offices, warehouses and rental units often have pipes running through unheated spaces, and a freeze can go unnoticed overnight or over a weekend. If you manage a commercial building, walk the cold spots on the first hard freeze of the season. If a fire sprinkler line freezes or leaks, call right away, because that system protects the building.
