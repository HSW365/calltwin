How Salons and Barbershops Can Handle the Holiday Booking Rush Without Missing Calls

From the week before Thanksgiving through New Year's Eve, everybody wants a fresh cut, a new color or a blowout at the same time. Your chairs fill up, your hands are full, and the phone keeps ringing. Here's how to plan for the rush so the calls you can't pick up still turn into bookings instead of going to the shop down the street.

Why the holidays are different

Most of the year, a missed call from a regular is no big deal. They know you, they'll text or try again tomorrow. During the holidays it changes. New clients are calling because their usual stylist is booked solid, people are trying to lock in a date before a party or a family photo, and everyone is calling several places at once. The shop that answers, or calls back first, usually gets the appointment.

At the same time, you're the busiest you'll be all year. Every minute you step away from the chair to grab the phone is a minute your client in the chair is waiting. So the goal isn't to answer every call yourself. It's to make sure every call gets caught and handled quickly.

Count what you missed last year

Before you plan, look back. Open your phone's call log, or your carrier's online account if it keeps history, and look at the same stretch from last year: mid-November through December. Count the calls you didn't answer during business hours, and the ones that came in after you closed. Skip spam and numbers you know were vendors.

Then do the math with your own numbers. Take the missed calls that were probably new or returning clients, multiply by your average ticket for a holiday service, and add the value of the clients who would have come back after the holidays. Your average ticket is easy to find: total service revenue for a month divided by the number of appointments. The result tells you how much a better phone plan is worth to you.

Open your holiday calendar early and say so

A lot of the December phone crush is people asking the same question: do you have anything before the 24th? You can cut that down by getting ahead of it.

  • Open holiday booking in early November. Tell your regulars in the chair, on a sign at the front desk and in a text to your client list.
  • Post your holiday hours everywhere. Your Google Business Profile, your booking page, your social accounts and your voicemail greeting. Make sure they all match.
  • Pre-book regulars before they leave. When a regular is in the chair in November, book their December appointment before they walk out. That's one less call later.
  • Decide on extra hours now. If you plan to open early, stay late or work a Sunday, put those slots on the calendar in advance so you're not inventing them on the phone.

Keep a waitlist and a cancellation list

When you're booked solid, the worst answer is just no. A caller you turn away is a caller who books somewhere else and may stay there. Keep a simple waitlist for each day: name, number, service and how much notice they need. When someone cancels, you text down the list until a spot fills.

This only works if you get the caller's details. A missed call with no message gives you nothing to put on the list. That's one more reason to make sure every call gets answered by someone or something that writes the details down.

Protect your busiest days with a deposit policy

No-shows hurt any time of year, but a no-show on the Saturday before Christmas is a slot you could have filled three times over. Many salons ask for a deposit or a card on file for holiday appointments, long services like color and extensions, or new clients.

If you go that route, keep it simple and say it clearly when the appointment is booked: how much the deposit is, whether it goes toward the service, and how much notice someone needs to give to get it back. Put the same policy on your booking page and in the confirmation text so nobody is surprised.

Your options for covering the phone during the rush

  • Answer between clients. Free, but you'll still miss every call that comes in while you're foiling, cutting or shaving, and callbacks hours later often reach someone who already booked elsewhere.
  • Push everyone to online booking. Great for regulars who like it. Plenty of callers still want to ask a question first, especially new clients and anyone booking for a group.
  • Hire seasonal front-desk help. A real person is great for walk-ins and checkout, but it's a wage for every hour, and nobody answers at 9pm when people are making their plans.
  • Use an answering service or an AI receptionist. Every call gets picked up, the details get written down, and the request is sent to you so you can confirm a time between clients.

Whatever you choose, write down what you need from each caller so nothing gets lost: name and number, the service they want, a preferred stylist or barber, the days and times that work, and whether they're new or returning.
