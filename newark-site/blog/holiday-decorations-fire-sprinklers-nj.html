Holiday Decorations and Fire Sprinklers: What NJ Businesses and Homeowners Should Know

The day after Thanksgiving is when garland goes up in storefronts, offices, restaurants, churches and apartment lobbies across Warren, Hunterdon, Sussex, Morris and Somerset counties. If the building has fire sprinklers, a few decorating habits can block them, damage them or set one off. Here is how to decorate without getting in the way of the system that protects the building.

Why decorations and sprinklers don't mix

A fire sprinkler head works on its own. Heat from a fire reaches the head, the heat-sensitive part inside it releases, and water sprays out in a pattern meant to cover the area below. Only the heads that get hot enough open. That design is what makes sprinklers effective, and it's also why they're easy to interfere with.

Anything that hangs on a head, wraps around it or sits close beneath it can do one of three things: keep heat from reaching it, block the spray once it opens, or bump the head hard enough to damage it. A damaged head can leak or go off with no fire at all, which in a store or office during the holidays means water on merchandise, ceilings and floors.

Never hang anything from a sprinkler head or its pipe

Sprinkler heads and exposed sprinkler pipe look like a handy place to tie a ribbon or hook a string of lights. They aren't. Keep these off of them completely:

  • Garland, wreaths, ribbons and bows
  • String lights and extension cords
  • Signs, banners and hanging ornaments
  • Tinsel, streamers and hooks or clips of any kind

Even light decorations put stress on the head and fittings, and the act of hanging and removing them is a common way heads get knocked. Use ceiling grid clips, wall hooks or freestanding displays away from the heads instead.

Keep the space under and around heads clear

Sprinklers need open space below them to spray properly. Fire codes require a clear area beneath heads, and your local fire official or sprinkler contractor can tell you what applies to your building. As a practical rule for the season:

  • Don't stack boxes of decorations, inventory or displays up toward the ceiling near heads.
  • Keep tall trees, inflatables and display towers from reaching up into the area under a head.
  • Don't build canopies, fabric ceilings or draped tents under sprinklers. They can hide the fire from the head and block the water.
  • Don't cover or bag heads to protect them from glitter, spray snow or paint. Covering a head can stop it from working.

If your back room or storage area fills up with holiday stock, take a minute to look up before you stack.

Don't paint, spray or coat the heads

Spray snow, window paint, glitter adhesive and decorative coatings shouldn't get on sprinkler heads. A coating on the heat-sensitive part can slow it down or keep it from opening. If a head has been painted or coated, don't try to scrub or chip it clean. Have a qualified sprinkler contractor look at it, because cleaning can damage it and a coated head may need to be replaced.

Watch heat sources near sprinkler heads

Sprinkler heads respond to heat, so keep heat-producing decorations and equipment away from them:

  • Older incandescent spotlights and display lighting aimed up at the ceiling
  • Portable heaters in entryways, booths or tents
  • Warming equipment, chafing dishes or cooking set up for holiday events under a head

Keep candles and open flames out of sprinklered areas where they could be knocked over or left burning. And as always with any gas appliance, heater or fireplace: if you smell gas, get everyone out and call your gas utility or 911 from outside, then call for service.

Don't block valves, risers and the fire department connection

The sprinkler control valve, riser and the fire department connection outside need to stay visible and reachable. Holiday displays, stacked deliveries and snow piles pushed up by plows can block them. Keep a clear path to the valve room or riser, and keep the outside connection free of decorations, planters and snow so firefighters can find and use it.

It's also a good time for whoever runs the building to confirm they know where the sprinkler shutoff is. If a head is damaged and starts flowing, knowing that valve can limit the water damage while you call for help.

Homes with residential sprinklers

More New Jersey homes, especially newer construction, have residential fire sprinklers in the ceilings. The same rules apply at home: don't hang lights or garland from heads, don't paint them, keep tall trees from crowding up against them, and keep ladders and decorating poles from bumping them. If you have kids or guests hanging decorations, point the heads out before anyone starts.

If a head is damaged, leaking or goes off

  • If there is any sign of fire, get everyone out and call 911. Let the system do its job.
  • If there is no fire and water is flowing, shut the sprinkler control valve only if you know which one it is and it is safe to reach. Call the fire department if you are unsure, and let them know the system is out of service.
  • Stay away from water near outlets, extension cords, light strings and electrical panels.
  • Don't hang decorations back up or reset anything yourself. A head that has opened or been damaged needs to be replaced and the system put back in service properly.

While the sprinkler system is shut off, the building isn't protected the way it should be, so get it repaired quickly.
